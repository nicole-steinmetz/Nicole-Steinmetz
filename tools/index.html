[ 01 ] How it fits

How the pieces fit. Platform, hosting, domain and DNS.

01

Platform

The software you log into to edit your site. Squarespace and Shopify include hosting.

02

Hosting

The computers that show your site to visitors. A custom-coded site needs its own host.

03

Domain

Your web address, such as yourbusiness.com.au.

04

DNS

The address book that tells the internet where your website and your email live.

One genuine gotcha: moving DNS to Cloudflare or Netlify can interrupt your email if the MX records aren't copied across first. It is a one-time step and carries no risk when handled properly, so I list your existing records before anything changes. See the FAQ on domain and email access.

[ 03 ] Platforms

Squarespace, Cloudflare and Shopify. These are the three I use.

Squarespace is best for managing the site yourself, and it doesn't accept custom code. Shopify is for products. Cloudflare is my preferred host for a custom static site, and this site is built on it. A custom build is hand-written code, so it needs a host. It has no visual editor, and I keep access to your site after launch for further changes. See the FAQ on updating your site after launch.

[ 05 ] Compare

The five platforms at a glance. Choose by the job.

Platform — Cloudflare

The infrastructure behind a custom build

Cloudflare is the network a hand-built site runs on — domain, hosting and delivery in one account you own, built to handle real traffic without extra setup.

See Cloudflare pricing
Worth knowing

This is infrastructure, not a client-run platform — someone has to own the account and the ongoing decisions. Moving DNS here can interrupt email if MX records aren't copied across first — a one-time step, and no risk when it's handled properly at launch.

Fast wherever they are

Served from the edge, close to every visitor — not from one data centre on the other side of the world.

Security built in

DDoS protection and SSL come with the platform, not as something to configure and hope for the best.

Genuine strength

Very fast delivery with flexible infrastructure

Core compromise

You are running a technical platform, not a self-contained CMS

Avoid when

You need low-skill, day-to-day ownership

Platform — Shopify

The commerce engine underneath

Once selling is the job, Shopify runs the parts that matter most — checkout, inventory, fulfilment — so the build can focus on how it looks and feels.

See Shopify pricing
Worth knowing

Shopify is a retail operating platform, not a finished storefront — expect to add apps as needs grow, and Shopify's own transaction fee applies if payments run through anything other than Shopify Payments.

Checkout that just works

A native, trusted checkout customers already recognise — one less reason to abandon a cart.

The commerce stack, already there

Inventory, shipping, discounts and payments are built in from day one, not bolted on later.

Genuine strength

Reliable hosted commerce with a strong admin

Core compromise

Polish often relies on paid apps and Shopify's conventions

Avoid when

Selling is only a minor feature

Platform — Squarespace

Built for hands-on control

Squarespace keeps editing in reach after launch — content, pages, pricing, all from the same screen the site was built in. Built by a Squarespace Platinum Expert (Top 1%), so the setup holds up long after handover.

See Squarespace pricing
Worth knowing

Squarespace buys speed and simple day-to-day ownership — it's a poorer fit if the business later needs it to behave like Shopify, a CRM or a custom app.

No developer on speed dial

Swap a photo, add a page, update a price — from the same screen the site was built in.

Design and hosting in one place

Templates, domain, hosting and editing all live under one login — nothing else to manage.

Genuine strength

Fast, low-maintenance sites you can update yourself

Core compromise

Limited data model, checkout and workflow control

Avoid when

The site is commerce-led, membership-heavy or needs deep integrations

Platform — Vercel and Netlify

A deploy pipeline for custom code

Netlify runs the other kind of custom build — lean hosting with a workflow built around fast, git-based deploys, so changes go out clean without the overhead of managing servers.

See Netlify pricing See Vercel pricing
Worth knowing

Netlify is excellent at publishing a custom site reliably — it isn't a full backend. Like Cloudflare, moving DNS here can interrupt email if records aren't copied across first.

Deploy without the drama

Every update goes out through the same clean pipeline — previewable before it ever touches the live site.

Built for the modern web

Plays well with the frameworks and tools a genuinely custom site is built from.

Custom builds

Which custom-build platform suits you?

All three host a hand-built site well. The difference is how your domain is set up — I'll recommend one, so you don't need to choose.

Cloudflare

Very fast delivery with flexible infrastructure

  • Forms: handled by a one-off form service, included in your build.
  • Avoid when: you need low-skill, day-to-day ownership.

Your domain's settings move to Cloudflare, so I review your email setup first.

Netlify

Keep your existing domain provider

  • Forms: built in, with no extra service needed.
  • Plan: most business sites need a paid plan so I can manage the site with you.
  • Avoid when: you expect no technical stewardship at all.

If the monthly allowance runs out, the site pauses — I set you up on the right plan from day one.

Vercel

For more app-like projects

  • Hosting: business sites need a paid plan.
  • Avoid when: you only need a simple site, where Cloudflare or Netlify will serve you well.

I only recommend Vercel when your project clearly needs it.

Hosting accounts are set up in your business's name, with access for me.
